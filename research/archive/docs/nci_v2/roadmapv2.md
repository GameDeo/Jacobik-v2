# Jacobik Roadmap v2: Transferable Statistical NCI and Conformer Generation

> **Date:** 2026-10-20  
> **Status:** Proposed implementation and evaluation specification; the new NCI model is not implemented or validated.  
> **Source:** Revised companion to [roadmap.md](roadmap.md). The original is preserved.  
> **Objective:** Improve fresh conformer generation through a smooth, data-derived NCI correction while retaining the statistical IK architecture.

## 1. Purpose, decisions, and evidence

NCI will remain one score component. Reference-versus-baseline enrichment supplies its initial geometric and chemical dependence. Offline full-complex energy and force measurements calibrate shared NCI parameters. The resulting tables transfer to new complexes through local descriptors; a production solve performs no energy calculation or fitting.

Terms may respond to the same motion. A poor coordination angle does not make a real contact disappear. This plan introduces no exclusive physical ownership, full cross-term covariance model, per-complex correction, or separate correlation-correction term. It starts with a sum over consistently enumerated occurrences. More complicated aggregation requires evidence of a specific failure.

### 1.1 What changes from v1

| v1 statement or omission | v2 decision and reason |
|---|---|
| Enrichment gives the exact well shape | Enrichment is a useful empirical starting surface; independent response measurements test its shape and strength. |
| `87.03` is a fresh pure-repulsion baseline | It is a stored-pool reranking result. Establish fresh controls before claiming generation gains. |
| Full-complex compliance determines NCI depth | Use compliance as a soft sharpness diagnostic/regularizer; fit incremental energy and force responses with the baseline present. |
| Exact context/bin needs ten observations from both sources | Use compatible context pooling and smooth spatial support. Sparse detailed contexts retain broader supported predictions. |
| Q2 owns co-bound contacts | Keep co-bound status as a descriptor; test coverage and transfer. Correlated geometry alone is not grounds for suppressing an interaction. |
| Mandatory competing-partner and methyl softmax rules | Start with additive unique occurrences. Saturation and competition models are conditional follow-ups. |
| HB-first architecture | Implement a common interface and exercise bond–atom, bond–ring, and ring–ring families. Activation remains evidence-dependent. |
| One-time permanent pose cache | Cache by generator, inputs, and environment. Search, embedding, baseline, and candidate-generation changes can invalidate reuse. |
| Fixed absolute performance and runtime promises | Separate measured history, proposed promotion policies, and optimistic targets. Measure costs on a pilot. |
| Task checklist without executable contracts | Specify artifacts, implementation boundaries, dependencies, and failure outcomes in Sections 3, 5, and 7. |

**Status terminology:** “Current” means verified in source or a named artifact. “Proposed” means the initial v2 design. “Conditional” means an extension justified only by a documented failure. Numerical policy choices below are proposed defaults, not measured physical constants; they must be frozen before validation results are opened.

## 2. Current architecture and mathematical conventions

### 2.1 Pipeline and frozen comparison contract

```text
Input graph, state, donors, requested coordination geometry
    → ETKDG / MMFF / GFN2 free-ligand embedding and supported E/Z enumeration
    → coordination assignments and symmetry reduction
    → kinematic trees, torsion-mode beam, allowed bends
    → joint Adam IK optimization with geometric constraints
    → seam handling, ranking, symmetry-aware deduplication, output window
```

The assembly optimizer uses statistical IK; ligand preparation does use MMFF and GFN2. Current typical settings include beam width 16, 200 Adam steps, learning rate 0.1, 0.5 Å heavy-atom deduplication, and a 200-raw-nat output window. These are versioned experimental settings, not mathematical invariants.

Freeze Q1–Q6, orientation, bend priors, BVS status, assignment logic, embedding, search budgets, output rules, and molecular state across the primary NCI comparison. Hash the full resolved profile, not just the name `u1`. S3/S4 changes get separate experiments and new baseline identities.

### 2.2 Score, sign, and units

For coordinates/kinematic state X and a fixed discrete assignment a, define the raw baseline reward

$$
B(X,a)=Q_1+Q_2+S_{\rm orient}+Q_5+Q_6+l_{\rm bend}.
$$

All contributions are in dimensionless score units (“nats”). Q6 and bend penalties are negative rewards. BVS is currently off; if a later experiment enables it, its raw QV contribution becomes part of that experiment's explicitly versioned baseline.

The proposed NCI reward is Aθ and

$$
S_\theta(X,a)=\frac{B(X,a)+A_\theta(X)}{Z},\qquad
E_0^{\rm eq}=-RTB,\qquad E_\theta^{\rm eq}=-RT(B+A_\theta).
$$

Use T = 298.15 K and R = 0.00831446261815324 kJ mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ for new calibration; preserve any legacy constants inside the frozen baseline. “Energy-equivalent” is a calibration convention, not a claim that the baseline is an exact thermodynamic energy. Positive NCI reward lowers this energy. **Undo `/Z` before converting score differences to kJ/mol.**

Z is the scorer's fixed feature-count normalization: max(Q1 count + active Q2 count + orientation count + Q5 count, 1), with the existing Q3/Q4 fallback when orientation tables are absent. Obtain it from `_agg_norm()`, not a separately reconstructed count. Dividing by a positive constant preserves the score argmax within that state; it does not by itself guarantee unchanged finite-step Adam/constraint dynamics.

Current `u1` uses thermal floors for both optimization and ranking (`rank_floor_fit=False`). Bend priors are implemented in the solver rather than solely in `Scorer.score`; evaluation must include their change when a motion changes bend coordinates. Augmented-Lagrangian constraint multipliers are algorithmic state, not energy labels. Use valid constrained motions for calibration, and report infeasible poses separately.

### 2.3 Existing quantities retained in the baseline

The following summarizes the active mathematics; source and frozen artifacts determine exact lookup ladders and fitted parameters. Sources: [scorer.py](src/jacobik/ik/scorer.py), [orientation.py](src/jacobik/stats/orientation.py), [angle_bends.py](src/jacobik/ik/angle_bends.py), [q6pmf.py](src/jacobik/stats/q6pmf.py).

**Q1: metal–donor distance.** A pooled Gaussian mixture supplies log p(d), with metal/state/donor/geometry context. Thermal donor SDs in Å are C 0.0406, P 0.0535, eta 0.0571, Cl 0.0577, O 0.0597, I 0.0598, Br 0.0650, S 0.0693, N 0.0705; unsupported elements use the existing fallback. A one-sided wall adds

$$
-\tfrac12[(d-d_{\rm hi})_+/s_{\rm wall}]^2.
$$

The wall onset uses the outermost positive-weight component's μ + 3 max(σfit, 0.05 Å); its scale is computed by `_q1_wall`. This wall is C¹ at onset, not globally C². The v2 C² requirement applies to the new NCI contribution.

**Q2: donor–metal–donor angles.** A pooled Student-t mixture models c = cos θ. For a component mean μc in cosine units, the thermal variance floor is

$$
v_T=(1-\mu_c^2)\sigma_\theta^2+\tfrac12\mu_c^2\sigma_\theta^4,
\quad \sigma_\theta=0.08609\,\mathrm{rad}.
$$

This corrects v1's ambiguous use of sin(μ) when the stored mean is already a cosine.

**Orientation.** Kappa donors use full-covariance mixtures of local bond-vector dot products in dimensions 1, 3, or 6. Each feature's cosine-space thermal floor is projected using the preceding formula with σθ = 0.07385 rad; the covariance receives the corresponding Loewner floor. A constant angle variance must not be inserted directly into every cosine feature. Haptic features are tilt, substituent lean, and metal-to-face distance dispersion (“slip”); eta2/eta3 use bend and slip, larger faces use all three. Current haptic covariances lack measured thermal floors. `COV_FLOOR=0.05**2` is fit-time additive covariance regularization; it is not a measured compliance bound.

**Q5: torsions.** Folded von Mises mixtures supply

$$
p(\phi)=\sum_k w_k\frac{\exp[\kappa_k\cos(\phi-\mu_k)]}{2\pi I_0(\kappa_k)},
\quad \kappa_k\le1/0.2402^2\approx17.3.
$$

**Q6: sterics.** The baseline retains repulsive-only runtime behavior. For eligible class-5 nonpolar pairs, the dimensionless shifted-force penalty is

$$
w(r)=\frac{A_{ij}}{RT}\{e^{-b_{ij}r}-e^{-b_{ij}r_m}
+b_{ij}e^{-b_{ij}r_m}(r-r_m)\},\quad r<r_m;
\quad w(r)=0\text{ otherwise}.
$$

Q6 contributes −w; Aij = √(AiAj), bij = (bi+bj)/2. Other eligible classes use their analytical onset cores, with linear continuation above the existing 50-kT threshold. Preserve class-specific eligibility and onsets; neither Q6 nor proposed NCI is accurately specified by one universal “>3 bonds” statement.

**Bend prior.** With angles in radians,

$$
l_{\rm bend}=-\frac1{2RT}\sum_j k_j[\max(0,|\beta_j|-\beta_{0j})]^2.
$$

Ordinary stiffnesses are 310/1100/1300/1800 kJ mol⁻¹ rad⁻² by bend type. Ordinary free zones are 2°; haptic free lean and special cases follow existing code. This penalty is generally C¹ at its free-zone boundary.

**QV/BVS: available, off.** Its implemented form uses V = Σ exp[(R0−d)/0.37] and a reference-ratio penalty −c(V/OS−μ)²/2 for supported states. Its revision remains a separate work item.

### 2.4 What curvature and ensemble measurements mean

For a specified one-dimensional motion X(t), compare the same energy units and sign:

$$
K_\theta=\frac{d^2E_\theta^{\rm eq}}{dt^2}
=-RT\frac{d^2(B+A_\theta)}{dt^2},\qquad
\rho_K=K_\theta/K_{\rm ref}.
$$

Report ratios only where positive reference curvature is resolved above numerical uncertainty; report wrong-sign cases separately. Historical E1 summaries use their original direction selection and ratio convention. Do not transfer them to every soft motion or silently invert/relabel them. Fixed scalar weights for existing terms remain unchanged; earlier global reweighting and correlated NNLS diagnostics failed or were unstable ([CHANGELOG](CHANGELOG.md), [architecture ledger](ARCHITECTURE.md)).

Outputs are ranked, deduplicated conformers, not calibrated thermal populations. Coordination assignments are discrete branches; continuous IK explores available motions inside each branch. Specified hapticity and fixed stereochemistry are input constraints, not automatically exhaustively enumerated chemical states. Additional ring-fold/stereochemical enumeration belongs to Section 4.

## 3. Work domain 1: transferable, data-derived NCI

### 3.1 Model contract and scope

“Arbitrary interaction” means a recurring nonbonded arrangement expressible by the selected local anchors, geometric features, and graph contexts. It does not imply recovery of every many-body electronic effect from pair geometry.

The initial shared implementation supports **bond–atom, bond–ring, and ring–ring** families. Each uses the same fitting, calibration, artifact, and runtime interfaces. At least two geometrically distinct families must complete the development procedure before calling the framework demonstrated; a failed family is a valid result and may remain inactive. Atom–atom, bond–bond, larger fragments, and additional environment descriptors are conditional extensions.

For occurrences o of family f with fixed graph context zo and continuous features xo(X),

$$
U_f(X)=\sum_{o\in\mathcal O_f}u_f(x_o(X),z_o),\qquad
A_\theta(X)=\sum_f\gamma_f U_f(X),\quad \gamma_f\ge0.
$$

First fit one common γ; permit regularized family scales only if grouped calibration cross-validation improves. Detailed context affects the surface from the start. Context-specific energy scales require additional evidence and are not part of the initial fit. No parameter is fitted to a complex identity.

Enrichment is an empirical proposal, not an exact well or a unique physical decomposition. A nonnegative reward can favor or disfavor a motion by gaining or losing attraction, but cannot represent every missing repulsive energy. If the frozen baseline requires an incompatible correction, record that limitation rather than force NCI to absorb it.

### 3.2 Shared anchors, features, and context

Let $A$ and $B$ denote endpoints of a covalent bond, $C$ an external atom, $c_R$ a ring centroid, $n_R$ its regularized unit normal, and $d=\hat u_\delta(c_S-c_R)$ a regularized separation vector. To make feature evaluation, radial norms, and ring frames globally $C^2$ and finite at degenerate geometries (before any taper is applied), define for any Cartesian displacement $v\in\mathbb R^3$:

$$
\lVert v\rVert_\delta=\sqrt{\lVert v\rVert^2+\delta_r^2},\qquad
\hat u_\delta(v)=\frac{v}{\lVert v\rVert_\delta},\qquad
\delta_r=10^{-4}\,\text{\AA}.
$$

For an ordered ring cycle $R=(i_1,\dots,i_m)$ ($m\in\{5,6\}$) with centroid $c_R=\frac1m\sum_{k=1}^m X_{i_k}$ and relative vertex vectors $q_k=X_{i_k}-c_R$, define the area vector and regularized unit normal:

$$
s_R=\sum_{k=1}^m(q_k\times q_{k+1}),\qquad
n_R=\frac{s_R}{\sqrt{\lVert s_R\rVert^2+\delta_n^2}},\qquad
\delta_n=10^{-6}\,\text{\AA}^2,
$$

where $q_{m+1}=q_1$. At $v=0$ or collinear ring collapse ($s_R=0$), $\hat u_\delta(0)=0$ and $n_R=0$ with finite, zero-valued first and second Cartesian derivatives. Extraction and runtime call the identical `features_torch` implementation; multiplying an undefined or `NaN` feature by a zero taper is not relied upon.

For `bond–atom`, let $\mathcal N_{\rm lig}(C)$ denote the covalent ligand neighbors $D\ne C$ of atom $C$ (excluding the metal atom; metal-binding status of $C$ is encoded in the discrete context $z_o$). When $k_C=|\mathcal N_{\rm lig}(C)|\ge 1$, define $c_D=\hat u_\delta(D-C)\cdot\hat u_\delta(B-C)$ and the fixed unweighted angular moments:

$$
\mu_1(C)=\frac1{k_C}\sum_{D\in\mathcal N_{\rm lig}(C)}c_D,\qquad
\mu_2(C)=\frac1{k_C}\sum_{D\in\mathcal N_{\rm lig}(C)}c_D^2.
$$

All covalent ligand neighbors (heavy atoms and hydrogens) are included with equal $1/k_C$ weight. When $k_C=0$ (for example, a monatomic halide ligand bound only to the metal), set a fixed graph-derived availability flag `has_c_neighbors = False`, set $\mu_1(C)=\mu_2(C)=0$, and evaluate a 2-feature $(r,\ \hat u_\delta(B-A)\cdot\hat u_\delta(C-B))$ surface for that context; never invent an angle. Pooling across $\mathcal N_{\rm lig}(C)$ fixes the continuous feature dimension at 4 while discarding azimuthal rotation around the $C$ cone and chemical distinctions among mixed substituents on an asymmetric acceptor (such as $sp^3$ $\text{O/N}$ with both $\text{C}$ and $\text{H}$ neighbors, or an ester/imine heteroatom). Discrete context $z_o$ records $k_C$, heavy-neighbor count, and the neighbor element/hybridization multiset; type-split or azimuthal descriptors remain conditional extensions justified only if calibration or validation residuals show systematic error on asymmetric acceptors.

These proposed feature definitions avoid `acos` and orientation-sign discontinuities and give every family a fixed continuous dimension $(\le 4)$:

| Family | Occurrence | Initial features ($x_o(X)$) |
|---|---|---|
| Bond–atom | Directed ligand bond $A\to B$ and external atom $C$ | $r=\lVert C-B\rVert_\delta$; $x_1=\hat u_\delta(B-A)\cdot\hat u_\delta(C-B)$; $x_2=\mu_1(C)$; $x_3=\mu_2(C)$ (2D $(r,x_1)$ when $k_C=0$) |
| Bond–ring | Directed ligand bond $A\to B$ and a graph ring $R$ | $r=\lVert c_R-B\rVert_\delta$; $x_1=\hat u_\delta(B-A)\cdot\hat u_\delta(c_R-B)$; $x_2=[n_R\cdot\hat u_\delta(B-c_R)]^2$; $x_3=[n_R\cdot\hat u_\delta(B-A)]^2$ |
| Ring–ring | Unordered distinct rings $R,S$ | $r=\lVert c_S-c_R\rVert_\delta$; $x_1=(n_R\cdot n_S)^2$; $x_2=(n_R\cdot d)^2+(n_S\cdot d)^2$; $x_3=(n_R\cdot d)^2(n_S\cdot d)^2$ |

For bond–ring, $\mathrm{height}^2 = r^2 x_2$ and $\mathrm{offset}^2 = r^2(1-x_2)$ are available for reporting. Ring–ring features permit parallel-displaced and T-shaped arrangements. Squared normal products make the representation invariant to normal reversal. Symmetric sums/products avoid sorting equivalent rings by instantaneous geometry.

Initial rings are unique graph cycles of length 5 or 6 with the existing $sp^2$-compatible atom/bond schema, explicitly versioned. A cyclic/reversed traversal of the same ring is one ring. Directed bonds with graph-distinct ends retain their direction; for graph-equivalent ends, average the two directional feature evaluations for one bond occurrence. This preserves label invariance without doubling its reward. Distinct atoms/contacts are not merged merely because they share an element or graph symmetry.

For the new candidate, participating anchors must be disjoint and have no ligand-graph path of $\le 4$ edges between any of their constituent atoms; paths through the metal are excluded from that graph. This conservative, explicit rule is versioned and differs in some intra-ligand cases from the historical H-to-acceptor-only test. Report that eligibility delta. Main-metal atoms are not standalone anchors in the first implementation; metal-bound ligand atoms/rings remain eligible.

**Bounded graph-based candidate admission.** Admitting every covalent bond against every external atom at $>4$ bonds separation would generate thousands of non-specific pairs per complex and risk cumulative compaction bias. Conversely, hardcoding a permanent H-bond-only filter would defeat the generic architecture. Therefore, `enumerate_opportunities` is table-driven by versioned graph-admission rules in `spec.json` (`schema.admission_rules`), keeping `bond–atom` restrictions distinct from `bond–ring` and `ring–ring`:

- **Bond–atom ($A\to B\cdots C$) initial pilot classes:**
  1. `polar_h_acceptor`: $A\in\{\text{N, O, F, S}\}$, $B=\text{H}$, and $C\in\{\text{N, O, F, S, Cl, Br, I}\}$ (or anionic/formal-negative $\text{C}$).
  2. `activated_ch_acceptor`: $A=\text{C}$, $B=\text{H}$ where $A$ is $sp/sp^2$-hybridized, directly bound to the metal, or bonded to $\ge 1$ electronegative/cationic heteroatom ($\text{N, O, F, S, P, Cl, Br, I}$), and $C\in\{\text{N, O, F, S, Cl, Br, I}\}$.

     > *Amended 2026-10-21 (§9.1 A1–A3):* split into `activated_ch_acceptor` ($sp$, metal-bound or heteroatom-bonded C) and a separate `sp2_ch_acceptor`; `polar_multiple_bond` uses bond order ≥ 1.4; level-1 context uses the endpoint descriptors.

  3. `sigma_hole_acceptor`: $A\in\{\text{C, N, O, S, P}\}$, $B\in\{\text{Cl, Br, I, S, Se}\}$, and $C\in\{\text{N, O, F, S, Cl, Br, I}\}$.
  4. `polar_multiple_bond`: $A=B$ or $A\equiv B$ hetero-multiple bond ($A\in\{\text{C, N, S, P}\}$, $B\in\{\text{O, N, S}\}$) with polar heteroatom partner $C\in\{\text{N, O, F, S, Cl, Br, I}\}$.
  Unactivated aliphatic $\text{C–C}$ and unpolarized $\text{C}(sp^3)\text{–H}\cdots\text{C}(sp^3)$ atom pairs are excluded from the initial `bond–atom` admission rules.
- **Bond–ring ($A\to B\cdots R$) initial pilot classes:** directed bonds $A\to B$ where $B=\text{H}$ (both polar $\text{D–H}$ and nonpolar/activated $\text{C–H}$, separated by context $z_o$) or $B\in\{\text{F, Cl, Br, I}\}$ (`lp_or_halogen_ring`), paired with any eligible 5- or 6-membered $sp^2$ ring $R$.
- **Ring–ring ($R,S$) initial pilot classes:** all unordered pairs of distinct eligible 5- or 6-membered $sp^2$ rings.

Adding or widening an admission class is a declarative specification change (`spec.json`) producing a new schema hash and requiring no solver or evaluator code edits. Bounded admission is a safeguard against non-specific pair summation, not a guarantee against over-attraction; actual occurrence counts $O_{pf}$, total rewards, and radial compaction diagnostics must be measured against complex size in Step 1.

Co-bound arrangements are included as a separately described context. The incumbent's co-bound exclusion remains part of the incumbent comparator only. Both directly bound status and haptic participation are determined from declared coordination sites, not instantaneous distance cutoffs. Adding co-bound opportunities does not guarantee that a fitted attraction for them will pass validation.

The context hierarchy is fixed before fitting:

1. Family + coarse anchor chemistry (admission class, endpoint element/charge class, bond order, ring aromaticity/composition) + metal-binding roles + intraligand/interligand flag + `has_c_neighbors` availability flag.
2. Add resonance/valence, $C$-neighbor count/hybridization summary, and typed one-hop graph environments.
3. Add metal family and declared coordination-state descriptors if support permits.

Pool detailed contexts toward compatible ancestors. Preserve charge class, anchor family, and binding role at the root; an unknown charged/bound environment must not silently inherit a neutral/free-contact table. An unseen whole complex can still contain many familiar local contexts.

No distance or angle threshold labels a positive contact. A computational neighbor list is allowed only if it contains every occurrence with nonzero score or derivatives at that step; for the first implementation, build the graph occurrence list once and evaluate it in batches.

### 3.3 Sampling, matching, and normalization

**Population.** Use the eligible cleaned tmQM X1–X3 and LnQM LNf frame with the official refit's benchmark exclusions and validity rules. Audit all shards, including the previously unscanned charged shards. Publish exact coverage; excluded shards narrow the named population.

**State.** A parent-state fixes whole ligand graphs, charge, multiplicity, protonation, fixed stereochemistry, donor identities, and declared binding modes. Coordination assignment is stored separately. For the initial density fit, match the reference assignment class when available, including only symmetry-equivalent assignments. Store other generated classes for diagnostics. This choice prevents enrichment from simply learning the wrong cis/trans population; inter-isomer transfer must then be tested explicitly. An unmatched assignment is recorded as missing matched data, not a negative contact. If assignment-matched coverage is poor, expand training generation by assignment or add assignment context as a new version before fitting.

**Allocation.** Start with 240 independent groups: 160 surface-fit, 40 energy-calibration, 40 untouched motion-validation. This is a budget allocation, not a proof of adequate coverage. Build groups from source siblings and shared informative ligand cores; define the scaffold algorithm in the manifest. Ubiquitous simple coligands such as isolated halides must not connect the entire corpus into one group. State which kinds of novelty the split does and does not establish.

> *Amended 2026-10-21 (§9.2 A4–A9):* transitive union-find is replaced by direct 1-hop benchmark quarantine plus pairwise-disjoint sampling; scaffold edges require a covalent distance; the common-ligand exemption is a frozen whitelist of graph keys; allocation is screened for solver mappability (LnQM is effectively excluded).

Sample groups without examining NCI scores or reference energies. Stratify by metal family, charge class, and coordination-number class where possible. Preserve zero-opportunity parents in the frame and evaluation. A separate graph-opportunity sample may enrich rare families; label it and report it separately. Population summaries of oversampled strata require recorded inclusion probabilities and inverse-probability weighting. Simple uniform group sampling needs no such correction.

**Poses.** Generate the frozen pure-repulsion control with seeds 11, 12, 13. Save all finite returned poses, exact settings, and failures. For fitting, use deduplicated assignment-matched poses with equal seed weight and equal pose weight within seed. Do not select controls by reference energy or a candidate NCI score. Store selected early/mid-optimization snapshots separately for calibration/coverage diagnostics, not as interchangeable final-pool observations.

**Matching.** Graph-constrained atom maps preserve donor roles and fixed stereochemistry; ambiguous mappings are flagged. Equivalent labelings of the same physical occurrence do not create extra rows. Distinct symmetry-related physical occurrences remain distinct. Reference and baseline extraction call the same feature implementation.

For parent $p$, family $f$ with $O_{pf}$ occurrences, and seed $s$ with $K_{ps}$ matched poses, one possible normalized row weighting is

$$
w^{\rm ref}_{po}=\frac{w_p}{O_{pf}},\qquad
w^{\rm base}_{psko}=\frac{w_p}{O_{pf}|S_p|K_{ps}}.
$$

Here $w_p$ is the group/population weight divided among sampled parent-states in that group. Both sources receive the same total parent/family mass. Renormalize the two class totals equally when fitting a density ratio. Parents with $O_{pf}=0$ have no rows for that family and remain in coverage/whole-system metrics. These training weights do **not** divide the runtime physical sum by occurrence count.

Report failures and unmatched states as outcomes. A predeclared reserve can replace missing training measurements within a stratum, but cannot erase original failures. Evaluation cohorts retain failures and never replace them after seeing outcomes.

### 3.4 Fit the statistical surface

For each family/context, the target is

$$
L_f(x,z)=\log\frac{p_w(x\mid\mathrm{reference},z)}
                         {p_w(x\mid\mathrm{baseline},z)},
$$

where $p_w$ denotes the weighted sampling distributions defined above. Use one set of coordinates and units for both sources; the geometric volume element then cancels in their ratio. Do not append the old random-placement correction to a matched solver-baseline ratio. Matched enrichment is an empirical proposal distribution contrast, not a guarantee of a true physical pair potential: apparent enrichment can reflect indirect kinematic correlations, and apparent depletion in the baseline can prevent precise density-ratio estimation until energy/force calibration is performed.

**Proposed estimator: regularized density-ratio classification.** Label reference rows $y=1$ and baseline rows $y=0$, balance total class weight ($\sum_{y_i=1}w_i=\sum_{y_i=0}w_i=\tfrac12$), and fit

$$
\min_\beta\ \sum_i w_i[\log(1+e^{F_\beta(x_i,z_i)})-y_iF_\beta(x_i,z_i)]
+\lambda_r\mathcal R_{\rm rough}(F_\beta)
+\lambda_0\|\beta_{\rm root}\|_2^2
+\lambda_z\sum_\ell\|\delta_\ell\|_2^2.
$$

With equal class priors, the unrestricted optimum is the log density ratio. Because second-derivative spline roughness penalties $\mathcal R_{\rm rough}(F_\beta)$ have a nullspace containing constant and linear functions of the coordinates—and because strongly attractive short-range wells may have zero or near-zero baseline occupancy ($y_i=0$ absent locally)—the explicit ridge penalty $\lambda_0\|\beta_{\rm root}\|_2^2$ on the root B-spline coefficients (alongside $\lambda_z\sum_\ell\|\delta_\ell\|_2^2$ on context refinements) is mandatory. It keeps the estimated log-odds finite in baseline-depleted neighborhoods where unpenalized logistic regression would diverge to $+\infty$. Absent baseline observations do not establish attraction strength; in such neighborhoods $F_\beta$ is bounded by $\lambda_0,\lambda_r$ and its physical strength is determined only by offline energy/force calibration (§3.7).

Use cubic B-splines with simple interior knots, which are $C^2$. In standardized feature coordinates, start with an additive radial term plus radial–orientation interactions:

$$
F_f(x,z)=b_f(r,z)+\sum_{k=1}^{D_f-1} b_{fk}(r,x_k,z).
$$

Center interaction bases to avoid duplicate intercepts. This controls dimensionality while permitting directional wells. Full three-feature interactions are conditional: add only when grouped fit-data validation demonstrates a reproducible missing dependency. Preserve the complete joint feature vector for support diagnostics even when the fitted function uses low-order interactions.

Implement partial pooling as a root surface plus penalized context corrections:

$$
F(x,z)=F_{\rm root}(x)+\sum_{\ell\in\mathrm{ancestors}(z)}m_\ell(x)\delta_\ell(x).
$$

The root is the compatible chemistry/binding-role group from Section 3.2. Context identity and coefficients are fixed within a solve; the spatial reliability functions $m_\ell$ are smooth and their derivatives are included. When a fine context lacks local evidence, its correction disappears and the root remains. This is one pooled prediction, not an additive reward for every ancestor.

Choose knot count and regularization by five-fold grouped cross-validation within the 160 surface-fit groups. Initial finite search: 4 or 6 interior knots per fitted coordinate, $\lambda_r, \lambda_z \in \{0.01, 0.1, 1, 10\}$, and $\lambda_0 \in \{10^{-3}, 10^{-2}, 10^{-1}\}$, after normalizing the data loss and basis coordinates. Prefer the simpler fit within one standard error of the best validation log loss. These are proposed numerical defaults. Root groups need at least 20 independent fit groups with opportunities in both sources; unsupported roots are recorded, not merged across incompatible chemistry just to reach a count.

### 3.5 Smooth support, positivity, and transfer during a solve

**Local support is a joint-neighborhood property, not an exact-bin requirement or a product of 1D/2D marginals.** Construct nonnegative tensor-product cubic B-spline basis functions $B_j(x)$ on a coarse **joint-feature** grid over the full feature vector $x=(r, x_1, \dots, x_{D_f-1})$. Their sum is one inside the declared feature domain. Do not replace the joint grid with a product of 2D $(r, x_k)$ support gates: two pairwise projections can each be populated while their joint 4D combination is physically unrealized. To keep the joint grid tractable in 4D, use a coarse support basis resolution (initial default: 6 basis intervals in $r$ within the active support window and 3 basis intervals per bounded angular coordinate $x_k$). If a family's joint grid remains too sparse on fit data, first coarsen the angular support basis (e.g., to 2 intervals) or reduce the feature representation before fitting; never fall back to an unrestricted extrapolating spline.

**Group-level support counting across both sources.** Multiple poses, seeds, or occurrences within an independent group $g$ never count as independent groups. For each joint basis function $B_j$ and source $s\in\{\mathrm{ref},\mathrm{base}\}$, define the group mass in neighborhood $j$:

$$
v_{g,j}^{(s)}=\sum_{i\in g,\,i\in s} w_i B_j(x_i),\qquad
N_j^{(s)}=\sum_g \mathbb I\!\left(v_{g,j}^{(s)}>\tau_{\rm grp}\right),\qquad
\mathrm{ESS}_j^{(s)}=\frac{\left(\sum_g v_{g,j}^{(s)}\right)^2}{\sum_g \left(v_{g,j}^{(s)}\right)^2},
$$

and the pooled group mass across both sources $v_{g,j}^{\rm pool}=v_{g,j}^{\rm ref}+v_{g,j}^{\rm base}$ with combined group count $N_j^{\rm pool}$ and effective group count $\mathrm{ESS}_j^{\rm pool}$ (where $\tau_{\rm grp}=10^{-3}\,\bar w_{\rm grp}$ prevents numerical tail leakage of a basis function from counting as group presence).

Because the pure-repulsion baseline actively avoids short-range attractive basins, a genuine interaction well can have strong reference support ($N_j^{\rm ref}\ge 10$) while local baseline occupancy is low or zero ($N_j^{\rm base}<10$). Zeroing $a_j$ whenever $N_j^{\rm base}<10$ would erase those short-range wells; conversely, treating absent baseline observations as proof of strong attraction would be invalid. Therefore, classify each joint neighborhood $j$ offline into one of three states and record both counts separately in the surface artifact:

1. **Matched-support neighborhood (`support_regime = "matched"`):** $N_j^{\rm ref}\ge 10$, $\mathrm{ESS}_j^{\rm ref}\ge 10$, $N_j^{\rm base}\ge 10$, and $\mathrm{ESS}_j^{\rm base}\ge 10$. Set $a_j=1$.
2. **Reference-supported, baseline-depleted neighborhood (`support_regime = "ref_only"`):** $N_j^{\rm ref}\ge 10$ and $\mathrm{ESS}_j^{\rm ref}\ge 10$, while $N_j^{\rm base}<10$ or $\mathrm{ESS}_j^{\rm base}<10$, provided the parent root/context has $\ge 20$ independent fit groups overall in both sources ($N_{\rm root}^{\rm pool}\ge 20$). Set $a_j=1$ so the well remains active, flag neighborhood $j$ in the artifact mask $M_{\rm ref\_only}$, and **require** that Step 4 calibration motions traverse the active `ref_only` region before the family/context can be promoted (see §3.7 and Gate 1).
3. **Unsupported neighborhood (`support_regime = "unsupported"`):** $N_j^{\rm ref}<10$ or $\mathrm{ESS}_j^{\rm ref}<10$. Set $a_j=0$ regardless of baseline occupancy.

Then

$$
m(x)=\sum_j a_jB_j(x)
$$

is $C^2$ and lies in $[0,1]$. Counts determine $a_j$ discontinuously **offline**; runtime varies smoothly with $x$. Freeze the support basis resolution and $a_j$ coefficients on fit data. For a root and its refinements, build $m_{\rm root}$ and $m_\ell$ from their respective support. A new detailed context can use a supported ancestor; an entirely unknown root contributes zero. Nearby unseen geometries inside supported neighborhoods receive attraction and gradients without requiring an exact complex or feature value to have appeared in training.

Replace the hard positive part by

$$
h_\epsilon(v)=
\begin{cases}
0,&v\le0,\\
\epsilon(6t^3-8t^4+3t^5),&0<v<\epsilon,\quad t=v/\epsilon,\\
v,&v\ge\epsilon.
\end{cases}
$$

The value and first two derivatives match at both joins. Start with $\epsilon=0.2$ nats, a numerical smoothing choice already represented in [the CTX prototype](tools/jdev/budget/nci_ctx1_model.py); reuse the mathematical function without carrying over its HB-specific selection/gating rules.

**Explicit inner and outer $C^2$ radial tapers.** Define the quintic step function $ S_5(t)=10t^3-15t^4+6t^5 $ for $t\in[0,1]$, and define both the short-range inner taper $T_{{\rm in},f}(r)$ and long-range outer cutoff $T_{{\rm out},f}(r)$:

$$
T_{{\rm in},f}(r)=S_5\!\left(\operatorname{clip}\!\left(\frac{r-r_{{\rm in,lo},f}}{r_{{\rm in,hi},f}-r_{{\rm in,lo},f}},0,1\right)\right),\qquad
T_{{\rm out},f}(r)=1-S_5\!\left(\operatorname{clip}\!\left(\frac{r-r_{{\rm on},f}}{r_{{\rm off},f}-r_{{\rm on},f}},0,1\right)\right).
$$

The complete runtime occurrence reward is

$$
u_f(x,z)=T_{{\rm in},f}(r)\,T_{{\rm out},f}(r)\,m_{{\rm root},f}(x)\,h_\epsilon(F_f(x,z)).
$$

Initial frozen radial windows $(r_{\rm in,lo}, r_{\rm in,hi}, r_{\rm on}, r_{\rm off})$ are:

- **Bond–atom ($B=\text{H}$):** $(1.10,\,1.35,\,6.0,\,8.0)\,\text{\AA}$
- **Bond–atom ($B\ne\text{H}$):** $(1.80,\,2.20,\,6.0,\,8.0)\,\text{\AA}$
- **Bond–ring:** $(1.60,\,2.00,\,6.0,\,8.0)\,\text{\AA}$
- **Ring–ring:** $(2.40,\,2.90,\,8.0,\,10.0)\,\text{\AA}$

The outer limits $(r_{\rm on}, r_{\rm off})$ are computational reach bounds, not positive-contact labels; actual learned support $m_{{\rm root},f}(x)$ is typically much shorter. The inner limits $(r_{\rm in,lo}, r_{\rm in,hi})$ ensure that $u_f(x,z)$ and its first two derivatives vanish identically below $r_{\rm in,lo}$, handing short-range steric exclusion cleanly to the frozen repulsive Q6 core while keeping all features $x(X)$ finite and $C^2$ for all $r\ge 0$ via $\delta_r,\delta_n$. Freeze all radial windows and $\epsilon,\delta_r,\delta_n$ in `spec.json` before calibration; changing them creates a new model version. Natural angular endpoints such as cosine $\pm 1$ or squared projections in $[0,1]$ are not tapered to zero: use natural/clamped B-spline boundary conditions on the bounded angular intervals and smooth polynomial invariants there.

Apply $h_\epsilon$ **after** evaluating the latent spline. This prevents interpolated negative rewards and avoids fitting a spline through already clipped bins. Smoothness must be verified for the complete Cartesian expression, including inner and outer tapers, joint support, context corrections, regularized norms, and ring-frame behavior.

**Confidence is separate from the runtime gate.** Grouped bootstrap estimates surface/scale stability for a finalist. A pointwise $L-1.96\,\mathrm{SE}$ is not a simultaneous confidence guarantee across all contexts or runtime trajectories. The proposed support/positivity rules are regularization policies, not “95% certain attraction” at each coordinate. Held-out prediction and generation are the acceptance evidence.

**Runtime sequence:** construct graph occurrences/context lookups once; at each IK step compute $x(X)$, evaluate the fixed surfaces, sum rewards, divide by $Z$ once, and differentiate. With optional environmental inputs $h(X)$, the full derivative is

$$
\nabla_Xu=(\partial u/\partial x)(\partial x/\partial X)
+(\partial u/\partial h)(\partial h/\partial X).
$$

The initial model has no extra $h(X)$. It does not switch chemical tables because a distance crosses a contact threshold. Geometry, not formal graph chemistry, normally changes during one fixed-state solve.

At a point where $T_{\rm in}T_{\rm out}m_{\rm root}$ is identically zero in a neighborhood, NCI supplies no force. Search and other score terms can bring a pose into range. If useful basins are absent from baseline support, use a labeled training-only motion sample to assess reach or route the problem to S3. Perturbed samples may be used for energy calibration immediately; adding them to the density-ratio denominator changes that distribution and requires explicit mixture weights and a new model ID.

### 3.6 Multiplicity and overlap: initial additive model

Use $A_\theta=\sum_f\gamma_f\sum_o u_o$. Enumerate each occurrence once; retain distinct physical occurrences. Overlapping descriptors and simultaneous Q1/Q2/Q5/Q6 responses are allowed. No “owner” suppresses a contact because another term also responds to its motion.

Fit all active family contributions together against the residual target below. Independent fits that each assign the full measured energy to a different descriptor can overcount it. Joint fitting reduces that problem but does not make correlated coefficients uniquely physical or guarantee that additive occurrences will never over-compact a large complex during fresh optimization. If scales are unstable, first pool them or remove a redundant descriptor.

Diagnostics include prediction error and total NCI reward/force versus opportunity count $O_{pf}$, heavy-atom count, radius of gyration / nonbonded contact count, partner count, and co-bound status. Only a reproducible count-dependent or size-compaction failure opens an aggregation experiment. Possible conditional alternatives include the previous zero-preserving log-sum-exp or a bounded sum $C[1-\exp(-\sum u/C)]$ for a justified competing group. Neither is an initial requirement; neither is a new score component. Any aggregation change requires recalibration and new validation.

### 3.7 Offline calibration: residuals are labels, not a runtime term

For a complex $p$, reference point $X_{p0}$, and controlled geometry $X_{pk}$ on a fixed-state motion, define

$$
y_{pk}=[E_{\rm ref}(X_{pk})-E_{\rm ref}(X_{p0})]
+RT[B(X_{pk})-B(X_{p0})],
$$

$$
D_{pk,f}=-RT[U_f(X_{pk})-U_f(X_{p0})].
$$

Thus $y$ is the energy change missing from the frozen baseline, $D\gamma$ is the proposed NCI energy change, and the fitted prediction is $\Delta E_0^{\rm eq}+D\gamma$. No residual is evaluated during production. No complex ID appears as a model feature or fitted parameter. Residual calibration anchors the average directional energy and force scale on sampled motions, but does not guarantee that out-of-path basins during global IK search cannot over-attract.

Use dimensionless motion parameter $t$ with a saved path $X_p(t)$; its physical amplitude is part of the path definition. For MACE force $F_{\rm ref}$,

$$
\frac{dE_{\rm ref}}{dt}=-F_{\rm ref}\cdot\frac{dX_p}{dt},\qquad
g_p=\frac{dE_{\rm ref}}{dt}+RT\frac{dB}{dt},\qquad
G_{p,f}=-RT\frac{dU_f}{dt}.
$$

Calibrate on energy changes and energy slopes using

$$
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\gamma\ge0}\quad&
\sum_p \frac{w_p}{n_p}\sum_k
  \left(\frac{(D\gamma)_{pk}-y_{pk}}{s_E}\right)^2\\
&+\lambda_F\sum_p\frac{w_p}{m_p}\sum_j
  \left(\frac{(G\gamma)_{pj}-g_{pj}}{s_F}\right)^2\\
&+\lambda_\gamma\sum_f(\gamma_f-\gamma_0)^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Weights sum to one over independent groups, with equal contributions within each group unless population weighting was declared. $n_p$ and $m_p$ prevent complexes with more measured motions from dominating. $s_E$ and $s_F$ are robust target scales computed on calibration-training folds only, each floored at 1 kJ/mol because $t$ is dimensionless. Record them; do not mix per-radian derivatives with per-$t$ derivatives. Start with $\lambda_F=1$ after this standardization. Compare energy-only fitting as a diagnostic.

First fit a single $\gamma_0$ shared across all families. For energy-only fitting without a prior, its explicit solution is

$$
\hat\gamma_0=\max\left(0,\frac{\sum_iw_iD_i y_i}{\sum_iw_iD_i^2}\right),
\quad D_i=\sum_fD_{i,f}.
$$

Zero/negligible denominator means the data do not identify an NCI scale. A zero optimum leaves that candidate inactive. It is not evidence that increasing attraction would help.

Next, compare regularized family scales against the shared fit using five-fold grouped cross-validation on the 40 calibration groups. Use $\lambda_\gamma\in\{0.1,1,10\}$; $\gamma_0$ is estimated inside each training fold. Choose the shared fit unless a family fit improves held-out standardized loss by more than one standard error. Refit the selected configuration on all 40 groups and freeze it before the separate 40-group validation. At least ten independent informative calibration groups must contribute to any separately fitted family scale; otherwise retain the shared scale and disclose limited family-specific evidence.

**Mandatory calibration coverage for baseline-depleted (`ref_only`) wells.** When an activated family or root surface relies on `support_regime = "ref_only"` neighborhoods (where $N_j^{\rm ref}\ge 10$ but $N_j^{\rm base}<10$), the uncalibrated spline height in that neighborhood reflects the ridge/roughness prior $\lambda_0,\lambda_r$ rather than a measured empirical density ratio. Therefore, any family/root whose active reward peak lies in a `ref_only` neighborhood must have at least 5 independent calibration groups with sampled motions that directly traverse that `ref_only` neighborhood ($|D_{pk,f}|>0.1\text{ kJ/mol}$ from occurrences in $M_{\rm ref\_only}$). If calibration motions do not reach the `ref_only` neighborhood, either add predeclared reference-centered approach/retreat calibration scans through that neighborhood before fitting $\gamma$, or set $a_j=0$ on the uncalibrated `ref_only` basis cells.

Report scale uncertainty and the singular values of the standardized $D/G$ design. Large coefficient variation under grouped resampling without stable prediction improvement indicates weak identification. Simplify rather than interpret those coefficients as separate physical energies. This addresses the [historical correlated-NNLS failure](CHANGELOG.md), without reweighting baseline terms or adding a covariance correction.

**Motion selection and cost.** For each calibration/validation parent, initially select up to three graph/geometry-defined informative IK motions before inspecting reference energies. Use the same selection procedure in both sets. First-pass amplitudes are 10° for a torsion or ligand rotation, 2° for an allowed bend, or 0.1 Å for an allowed translation; parameter $t$ ranges over $\{−1,−0.5,0,0.5,1\}$. Reject geometrically infeasible paths by the frozen independent validity rules and record them; do not reject because an energy is unfavorable. Use reference and baseline centers where available, and separately labeled early/mid-solve centers for trajectory diagnostics. Covalent internal coordinates outside the solver's degrees of freedom are not calibration motions.

A five-point scan estimates local shape only. Add one predeclared wider approach/retreat scan per family on graph-selected calibration cases to inspect shoulders, `ref_only` short-range wells, and the onset of repulsion; freeze amplitudes before labeling. Measure forces at scan centers and compare their projections with finite differences. MACE inputs retain the full coordinated complex, its charge, and multiplicity. Each displaced single point needs no fresh solve. Count all centers, displacements, wider scans, and validation evaluations in the budget.

### 3.8 Thermal information and limited shape refinement

Raw cached Hessians are under `data/refit/thermal/hess/`; [thermal_widths.json](data/refit/final/thermal_widths.json) contains summary widths, not those matrices. The historical cohort contains 299 successfully summarized complexes. For a valid Hessian H in kJ mol⁻¹ Å⁻² and feature Jacobian J,

$$
C_X=RT H^+,\qquad C_x=JC_XJ^T.
$$

Rigid/negative/near-zero mode handling, original geometry, atom ordering, and units must be recorded. Covariance of multiple features is a matrix. First-order projection can fail at symmetric extrema of cosine/squared features; use finite-displacement or second-order checks, or mark the width unavailable. Missing widths are not inferred as zero.

The inverse projected covariance describes a local **full-complex** constraint. It does not isolate the stiffness or depth of an added NCI term. The [existing thermal audit](tools/jdev/budget/nci_thermal_audit.py) explicitly records this limitation.

Primary smoothness control is the standardized spline roughness penalty. Where trustworthy Cx exists, a possible additional soft sharpness penalty is

$$
\mathcal R_T=\sum_v
\left\|\left[C_x^{1/2}(-\nabla_x^2 u_v)C_x^{1/2}-\kappa_{\max}I\right]_+\right\|_F^2,
$$

where [·]+ keeps positive eigenvalues and κmax=1 is an initial diagnostic reference. The product is dimensionless. This is a configurable regularizer against excessively sharp reward maxima, not a formula for an attraction amplitude or an exact physical ceiling. Its weight is selected on fit/calibration data; absent or unreliable compliance contributes no such penalty. Recheck sharpness after applying fitted γ, and validate **total** energy curvature along the same motions. Never divide a positive physical stiffness by the negative Hessian of a reward maximum and interpret the result as a positive scale.

If an amplitude fits depth changes but fails force/curvature transfer, allow one controlled shape extension on development data: a smooth radial coordinate dilation about a fixed empirical peak, with width factor b constrained initially to [0.8,1.25] and a penalty on (log b)². Keep the original physical support/cutoff functions in physical coordinates; verify the transformed latent evaluation remains within its fitted domain. Freeze the extension before new validation. If this is insufficient, a penalized low-order spline residual is a separate candidate version. Neither extension is automatic, and examined validation data become development data.

## 5. Validation protocol and decision rules

### 5.1 Preflight: freeze comparisons and establish repeatability

The primary fresh arms are:

- **Control:** frozen `u1`, repulsive Q6, NCI off.
- **Incumbent:** same resolved settings with the deployed HB-NCI component.
- **Candidate:** same settings with the new calibrated NCI component replacing incumbent HB.

Use matching seeds, input states, output budgets, device settings, and evaluator. Save/reuse identical embedding and initialization inputs where possible. Enable deterministic embedding in all arms if required for repeatability, and record that change from historical settings. It does not modify the deployed default during research.

On six size/chemistry-diverse parents from the development pilot, repeat the control twice for each of seeds 11–13. Initial tolerance: absolute difference in each parent's seed-mean top-1 and pool floor ≤1 kJ/mol, and no individual seed difference >5 kJ/mol. If violated, stabilize/cache initialization and rerun that repeatability check, or design a larger repeated-run comparison before evaluating candidates. Do not interpret a 1–3 kJ/mol gain using an unresolved 50-kJ/mol control variation.

Freeze the model, candidate-family activation, cohort IDs, selection rules, primary metrics, bootstrap settings, exclusion rules, numerical tolerances, and runtime budget in `evaluation_contract.json`. Values below are the initial proposed contract. Changes after looking at a validation result create a new development iteration and require fresh independent validation for the changed claim.

### 5.2 Metric definitions

For method m, parent p, seed s, fixed reference Rp, and output pool Pmps:

$$
\begin{aligned}
e_{mps}&=E_{\rm ref}(X^{\rm native\ top1}_{mps})-E_{\rm ref}(R_p),\\
f_{mps}&=\min_{X\in P^{\rm valid}_{mps}}
  [E_{\rm ref}(X)-E_{\rm ref}(R_p)],\\
r_{mps}&=e_{mps}-f_{mps}.
\end{aligned}
$$

e is a signed reference gap; a negative value is allowed and is not automatically a structural recovery success. f measures generation under the fixed budget. r measures selection regret when native top-1 is valid. Native top-1 is never silently replaced by a lower-ranked valid structure. Record invalid/empty/failed outputs first; energies and regret for unevaluable outputs are missing with a reason, not assigned a favorable value.

For a comparator b, define the parent-level change

$$
d_p^{(e)}=\frac13\sum_{s=11}^{13}(e_{{\rm cand},ps}-e_{b,ps}),
$$

and analogously for floor and regret. Negative is favorable. Compute confidence intervals by resampling independent groups, keeping parents and seeds together; use 4,000 bootstrap resamples with saved seed 0. Report pairwise complete-case energy summaries alongside every failure; eligibility for promotion requires no new parent-level no-output/invalid-top1 failure relative to either comparator. Partial failures cannot improve the headline result through denominator changes.

Also report:

- Valid native top-1 share, valid-output coverage, and invalid fraction under independent graph/state/geometry checks adapted from [EVALUATION_DESIGN.md](benchmarks/EVALUATION_DESIGN.md).
- Top-1 within 4.184 kJ/mol of valid pool floor; fixed-K best energy/coverage in addition to the complete returned pool.
- Parent mean/median changes, number of parent mean losses >10 kJ/mol, worst seed loss, and tail distributions.
- Seed range max(e)−min(e). The historical table used max(e)−median(e); label both distinctly.
- Within-assignment and between-assignment ranking correlations against **negative energy**, with minimum sample counts and undefined cases disclosed.
- Reference geometry/core recovery and binding-mode validity, independently of energy ranking.
- Mean/p95 generation wall time, peak memory, and opportunity count; energy-evaluation time separately.

Metrics such as “>1000 kJ/mol” are energy-tail diagnostics, not substitutes for chemical validity. Across different arms, median(e) need not equal median(f)+median(r).

| Gate | Measurements | Initial acceptance rule |

| **1. ### 5.4 Failure outcomes and scope control

Every gate returns `pass`, `fail`, or `inconclusive`, naming the criterion and evidence. A representation/coverage failure, zero scale, or adverse generation result is a useful research outcome. It does not authorize a global reweighting or an extra correction term.

After failure, the examined data become development diagnostics. A revised model has a new ID and an explicit changed hypothesis. Dropping a failing family after viewing validation is model selection; it does not preserve that set's unseen status. Keep one active candidate specification at a time and document unsuccessful alternatives in the registry.

### 6.2 Defensible planning scenarios

For positive improvement G, an approximate accounting identity is

$$
G\approx p_{\rm helped}g_{\rm helped}-p_{\rm harmed}\ell_{\rm harmed}.
$$

| Illustrative scenario | Helped fraction | Mean improvement there | Overall mean improvement before losses |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Limited reach | 10% | 10 kJ/mol | 1 kJ/mol |
| Useful broader reach | 20% | 15 kJ/mol | 3 kJ/mol |
| Strong broad effect | 30% | 20 kJ/mol | 6 kJ/mol |

These are conditional arithmetic scenarios, not probability forecasts. Two 40-kJ/mol losses among 76 parents erase about 1.05 kJ/mol of mean benefit. A small affected subset can improve the mean without moving the median. Pool-floor and regret changes must be measured separately.

## 8. Immediate next action and rationale

The next implementation task is **Steps 0–1: evidence registry, corpus/frame definition, and a bounded generic-feature pilot**. Use that work to freeze a feasible sample and numerical contract before launching the full 240-group library. The first milestone is a reproducible, general interface and trustworthy control, not a numerical promise of improved dev80 energy.

The main hypotheses are explicit:

1. Matched structural enrichment contains useful, transferable interaction geometry.
2. A compact shared correction can improve residual energy/force response without absorbing unrelated baseline errors.
3. That improvement survives fresh optimization, including unfamiliar complexes and competing geometries.

Each can fail independently. Surface fitting tests the first; independent motion response tests the second; fresh generation tests the third. A passing earlier stage does not imply a later one will pass.

Methodological precedent supports trying this approach: [DARS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2567923/) uses decoys as a statistical reference; [DOPE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2242414/) explains the assumptions behind statistical potentials; [orientation-dependent SOAP](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3842762/) demonstrates reusable orientation descriptors; [force matching](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18601325/) motivates fitting energy derivatives in a restricted model class. Their results are not evidence of success on these metal complexes. This plan borrows the estimation principles without claiming an equilibrium Boltzmann distribution or a unique physical energy decomposition for Jacobik's generated pools.

## 9. Amendments from Steps 0–1 execution (2026-10-21)

These amendments record where the executed Steps 0–1 deliberately depart from the text above, and why. The original sections are left as written; where they disagree, this section governs. Frozen artifacts: `data/refit/nci_v2/pilot_v1/spec.json` (`2.0.0-pilot.3`, sha256 `9c0870fc…`) and `frame.json` (sha256 `aa4292e3…`). Each spec change is listed in the spec's `revision_notes`.

### 9.1 Admission rules (amends §3.2)

**A1. C–H acceptor split.** §3.2 defines one `activated_ch_acceptor` class covering $sp/sp^2$ donors. It is split in two:
- `activated_ch_acceptor`: donor C that is $sp$ (degree ≤ 2), metal-bound, or bonded to a heteroatom in {N, O, F, S, P, Cl, Br, I}.
- `sp2_ch_acceptor`: unactivated $sp^2$ C–H (degree 3, not already activated), with the same acceptor set. It has its own root context, support counts and calibration accounting.

*Why.* Both rules pass the occurrence thresholds, so this was not needed for scaling. The reason is modeling: unactivated aryl C–H rows outnumber activated donors and would dominate a shared root context. That would mix weak and strong donors in one surface and make the class's calibration reflect mostly aryl C–H. As a separate class, the aryl rows can be fit, calibrated, or left inactive independently. (An earlier justification, "covered by `ch_ring`", was wrong: `ch_ring` is C–H to ring face, not C–H to heteroatom.) Pilot.3 counts on the 172 dev/fit complexes:

| Class | Median | p95 |
|---|---|---|
| `activated_ch_acceptor` | 12 | 94 |
| `sp2_ch_acceptor` | 32 | 157 |

**A2. `polar_multiple_bond` bond-order threshold is 1.4,** not 1.5. This matches the `double` bond-order class boundary used in the context key (§3.2 level 1), so admission and context use one definition.

**A3. Level-1 root context** uses the spec descriptors: endpoint elements, endpoint formal-charge classes, bond-order class, ring composition, metal-binding roles, intra/inter-ligand flag and `has_c_neighbors`. It no longer uses whole-complex charge. *Risk to check in the Step 3 support analysis:* this root is fine-grained. Report support per root context and pool to ancestors where groups are insufficient.

### 9.2 Population, grouping and leakage (amends §3.3 and §7.1 Step 0)

**A4. Direct 1-hop benchmark quarantine replaces transitive union-find.** The §7.1 recipe (union-find over siblings plus shared scaffolds) chained one giant component of 28,734 complexes. That component touched a benchmark, so 29,079 complexes (23.1%) were excluded.

The frame now excludes only three kinds of complex:
- the benchmark complexes themselves,
- their source siblings,
- complexes that directly share a non-exempt scaffold with a benchmark complex.

Splits are then drawn by sequential sampling that rejects any candidate sharing a sibling family or a non-exempt scaffold with the benchmarks, the pilot, or another split. Union-find is run only within each split, to set weights $w_g = 1/N_g$.

*Why.* Leakage means a test complex shares identity (sibling or scaffold) with training data. A chain A–B–C, where A and C share nothing, carries no information about C into A. Transitive closure therefore excluded a quarter of the corpus without reducing leakage. Pairwise disjointness across splits is enforced directly, and the leakage counts in `frame.json` are computed, not asserted.

**A5. Scaffold-key definition.** A ligand's key is its heavy-atom formula plus an element-labelled Weisfeiler–Lehman hash of the metal-free ligand component. A ligand edge now requires both a Wiberg bond order ≥ 0.08 and a distance ≤ 1.25·(r_i + r_j) + 0.25 Å (the distance check is the amendment). This is the same distance cutoff as `jacobik.stats.mapping`'s bond sanity check.

*Why.* Wiberg ≥ 0.08 alone also admits 1,3 and 1,4 contacts inside aromatic rings. That splits one ligand across several keys, under-grouping the corpus and hiding real benchmark overlaps:
- PPh3 appeared under at least four keys (one with 7,564 families, another with 652); with the distance check it is one key with 8,657 families.
- Distinct keys fall from 61,011 to 46,879.

The key ignores H atoms and bond orders. So PPh3/PCy3, benzene/cyclohexane (and therefore phenyl), IMes/SIMes and IPr/SIPr share a key, and benzyl shares tolyl's. This is conservative for grouping.

**A6. Common-ligand exemption is an explicit frozen whitelist, not a frequency rule.** The first frame exempted every ligand found in more than 50 sibling families (244 ligands). That swept in identity-defining scaffolds (porphyrins, Tp, Xantphos, triphosphines), and the cutoff was a cliff: 52 ligands were exempt at 51–60 families, while 59 at 41–50 were grouped.

The spec now freezes 44 named ligands, resolved to graph keys (`frozen_graph_keys`: 41 distinct keys, all present in the corpus). Each key is re-derived from its SMILES on every frame build and asserted. Rules:
- **Default:** any ligand not on the list is grouped.
- **Never exempt:** macrocycles; Tp/Tp*; pincers; terpyridine; salens; tri- and tetraphosphines; any ligand with ≥ 3 donor atoms (except η5-Cp-type); substituted Cps other than Cp*.
- **Still modeled:** exempt ligands are only exempt from scaffold grouping. They stay fully modeled in every NCI family.

Five entries were added after measuring which ligands drove quarantine under the corrected keys: benzoate, benzyl, C6F5, dmpe and 1-methylimidazole. Each was verified by SMILES-to-key match, not by formula. The sixth driver, a C6 key, is methylcyclopentadienyl, so it stays grouped under the substituted-Cp rule.

Result: 7,220 complexes quarantined (5.7%) and 118,590 eligible. Without the five additions the quarantine was 11,283.

**A7. The RDKit SMILES/InChIKey check is a second-hash check, not an independent leakage audit.** It uses the same heavy-atom fragments and exempt set as the WL keys, so it guards against hash collisions only. It is labelled as such in `frame.json`.

**A8. Solver mappability is screened before allocation.**
- For each stratum, the first 20 candidates in hash order are checked with `ligand_inputs_from_mol`.
- Strata are allocated in proportion to their estimated mappable population.
- During sampling, unmappable candidates are recorded per stratum and skipped.
- Strata with no mappable candidate are excluded and recorded.
- Inclusion probabilities are relative to the estimated mappable sub-population.

*Why.* Evaluation cohorts never replace failures (§5.1). Allocating unmappable groups would waste fixed evaluation slots and turn input limitations into unreported attrition. A declared narrowing is preferable to silent attrition. Solver failures after allocation are still recorded as outcomes; reserve replacement remains training-only, within stratum and in predeclared order.

### 9.8 Carried into Steps 3–4 (2026-10-24)

These amend §3–§5 for the remaining steps. They come from the Step 2 audit, the S2.1 repair, and the Steps 3–4 development work on branch `codex/nci-v2-steps3-4`.

- **Gate G4 is narrower than recorded in §9.5.** The G4 drift tolerances are met on top-1 and floor energies, but the GPU path is not bit-reproducible, even within one commit. Repeated solves can change conformer counts (RIQDEB s11: 16 vs 15) and move non-top-1 conformers by several Å (FANTIZ s11: 7.9 Å). Any arm comparison (Gates 4–5) therefore also runs a **control-vs-control repeat** on the same cohort. An arm effect inside that repeat's spread is not evidence.
- **OOM handling (amends §9.5 G5 O3).** The `max_rows` retry ladder splits work by seed only, so it cannot shrink one seed's batch. Its retries ran inside the exception handler, which kept the failed attempt's CUDA-graph memory pinned (fixed `21d5446`). `--mem-fraction` was ignored (fixed `838d843`). Step 2's 15 training OOMs were largely this bug, so its reserve replacements carry an artifactual size bias. The surface-fit cohort restores the slots that succeeded on retry (`repair_manifest.json` `slots`). A sensitivity fit on the unrestored Step 2 occupants is reported alongside it.
- **Health-conditioned density (clarifies §3.3).** Step 3 fits reference-vs-baseline enrichment on health-passing poses only: coordination intact, no stray H/F, no heavy-atom overlap, covalent geometry within the frozen policy. It uses the thresholds frozen before Step 2 access (`refit/nci_v2_controls_policy.json`, `solver-artifact-controls.2`). On development pools this removes about 48% of poses, mostly for coordination loss. The fitted term is defined relative to that conditioned baseline.
- **Binned fit (amends §3.4 compute).** The exact fit is infeasible at production scale (≈77 h at 20M rows). Production uses the exact-mass-preserving binned loss. Its CV choice matched the exact and float64 fits on synthetic data (rank correlation 0.99996). The same check must pass on a real-data subset before the full fit.
- **S2b: evaluation pools with solver state (blocks Step 4).** The bend prior is a material part of the baseline score B(X). Dropping it changes within-pool order in 29 of 54 development pools and top-1 in 4. Pools do not store the solver internal coordinates needed to evaluate it at displaced geometries. Before Step 4, the orchestrator saves per-pose `thetas_final` with trial, assignment and embed indices. The 240 calibration/validation complexes are then regenerated as a new, separately registered artifact. Step 3 is unaffected.
- **Expected Step 4 coverage.** Native solver motions traverse inter-ligand contacts well, but intra-ligand contacts poorly (baseline 0–15% vs reference 13–31% on development pools). Intra-ligand families may end without supported calibration; §3.2 allows that outcome.
